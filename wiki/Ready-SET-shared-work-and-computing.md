[Ready S.E.T.](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/ready-set-co-op-cultural-intelligence-node) means Sustainable Employment and Training. The proposal gives the local programme a way to organise work, build practical capability and share resources over time.

The [Aura disaster resilience work](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/aura-disaster-resilience) proposes a shared [Working Together handbook](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/aura-disaster-resilience/blob/main/wiki/Working-together.md) for local teams and community coordination.

The cooperative is also a possible intermediary. It could arrange shared equipment, learning support, administration and relationships between small teams that would otherwise repeat the same work. Each service needs an actual provider and budget. Paid responsibility, volunteering and [C-Hour](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/Paul-overview-handover/wiki/C-Hours-and-reciprocity) recognition remain clearly described.

## The Cultural Intelligence Node

The [Ready S.E.T. Co-op Cultural Intelligence Node](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/ready-set-co-op-cultural-intelligence-node) is the proposal I discussed with [Julie Talty](https://www.redland.qld.gov.au/About-Council/Mayor-and-Councillors/Mayor-and-councillor-profiles/Division-6-Mt-Cotton-Redland-Bay). It links local computing, a community living twin, learning, cultural work and shared services.

A node is a place and an organised capability. It could support AI-assisted work, simulation, media production, local records and training. Culture remains with the people and custodians concerned. A computer does not acquire authority over a story because it has been given a copy.

The current computing reference includes a GB200 NVL72-class rack. That is a reference for exploring workloads, power, cooling, space, support and cost. There is no purchased rack or operating data centre established by the proposal. The first useful workload and the people to support it come before choosing the final size of the installation.

## An ordinary Tuesday and a disrupted Tuesday

| Function | Ordinary use | During a disruption |
|---|---|---|
| Local information | Events, services, project records and practical learning | Dated official advice, local service status and verified needs |
| Shared work | Paid tasks, training, volunteering and maintenance | Agreed support tasks within the team's actual capability |
| Computing | Creative production, useful AI tools and simulations | Essential local records and communication, where power and connectivity allow |
| Places and equipment | Workshops, gatherings, accessible support and shared resources | Their agreed role in the local response, with responsible operators |
| Review | Learn from routine delivery and costs | Record gaps and improve preparedness after the event |

## Council and emergency arrangements

Redland's formal disaster arrangements include the Local Disaster Management Group, Local Disaster Coordinator and Local Disaster Coordination Centre. The cooperative would sit within agreed community relationships with those arrangements. [The council integration chapter](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/aura-disaster-resilience/wiki/Council-integration) explains the intended relationship.

An interface to Guardians IMS or another coordination system is a proposed technical and organisational connection. Access, information handling, responsibility and support would need agreement with the system's actual operators.

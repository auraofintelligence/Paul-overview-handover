Ready S.E.T. means Sustainable Employment and Training. The proposal gives the local programme a way to organise work, build practical capability and share resources over time.

It is the operating connection between a person's interests, a useful local task, the people able to support it and the resources needed to finish it. The proposed cooperative would have its own members, governance, finances and employed or contracted roles. It is a structure to develop with participants, rather than an organisation already operating all the activities described here.

## Start with the work people actually need

A local team might support an older resident to use a service, organise a film event, maintain shared equipment, help a learner prepare for paid work or document a community project. Each task needs a person responsible, a realistic scope and a way to know it has been completed.

The [Aura disaster resilience work](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/aura-disaster-resilience) proposes a shared working handbook and local team agreements. These would describe how people join, what they take responsibility for, how they raise a concern, and how responsibility passes to someone else. A handbook becomes useful through real practice and correction, rather than by accumulating rules nobody uses.

The cooperative is also a possible intermediary. It could arrange shared equipment, learning support, administration and relationships between small teams that would otherwise repeat the same work. Each service needs an actual provider and budget. Paid responsibility, volunteering and C-Hour recognition remain clearly described.

## The Cultural Intelligence Node

The [Ready S.E.T. Co-op Cultural Intelligence Node](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/ready-set-co-op-cultural-intelligence-node) is the proposal I discussed with Julie Talty. It links local computing, a community living twin, learning, cultural work and shared services.

A node is a place and an organised capability. It could support AI-assisted work, simulation, media production, local records and training. Culture remains with the people and custodians concerned. A computer does not acquire authority over a story because it has been given a copy.

The current computing reference includes a GB200 NVL72-class rack. That is a reference for exploring workloads, power, cooling, space, support and cost. There is no purchased rack or operating data centre established by the proposal. The first useful workload and the people to support it come before choosing the final size of the installation.

Smaller equipment, shared remote services or a staged combination may answer early needs. The same questions apply at each scale: what runs here, who uses it, what information it receives, what it costs and who fixes it when it stops.

## An ordinary Tuesday and a disrupted Tuesday

| Function | Ordinary use | During a disruption |
|---|---|---|
| Local information | Events, services, project records and practical learning | Dated official advice, local service status and verified needs |
| Shared work | Paid tasks, training, volunteering and maintenance | Agreed support tasks within the team's actual capability |
| Computing | Creative production, useful AI tools and simulations | Essential local records and communication, where power and connectivity allow |
| Places and equipment | Workshops, gatherings, accessible support and shared resources | Their agreed role in the local response, with responsible operators |
| Review | Learn from routine delivery and costs | Record gaps and improve preparedness after the event |

The value of the comparison is that preparedness grows from capability people already use. An unfamiliar system introduced during an emergency has a harder job.

## Council and emergency arrangements

Redland's formal disaster arrangements include the Local Disaster Management Group, Local Disaster Coordinator and Local Disaster Coordination Centre. The cooperative would sit within agreed community relationships with those arrangements. It would not acquire their powers through a software platform. [The council integration chapter](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/aura-disaster-resilience/wiki/Council-integration) explains the intended relationship.

An interface to Guardians IMS or another coordination system is a proposed technical and organisational connection. Access, information handling, responsibility and support would need agreement with the system's actual operators.

## What a local start could establish

A useful first version would have a named organising group, a short list of services people want, one host or working location, and a person paid or otherwise explicitly supported to coordinate the work. A basic operating record would show tasks, money, time, equipment, permissions and outcomes.

For you, these are possible inputs alongside your existing work. You might follow a connection, question the proposal or introduce somebody with relevant experience. The computing node is one path through the wider architecture, with further involvement chosen by the people interested in developing it.

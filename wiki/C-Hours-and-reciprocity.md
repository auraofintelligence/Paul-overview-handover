C-Hours record freely given human contribution. The intended meaning has always been reciprocal. Earlier AI-assisted material sometimes described them in commercial terms that do not represent the design.

One C-Hour means one verified hour of voluntary human contribution. It has no dollar value, fixed exchange rate, monetary backing or promise of redemption. It is not a transferable currency, an investment, an ownership share or a substitute for wages. The community decides freely how it expresses appreciation and what opportunities it offers.

This definition governs the handover, including where older documents use a different vocabulary. ## What a contribution record does

A contribution record makes useful work easier to see. Someone helps set up an event, assists a neighbour, contributes to a community archive, mentors a learner or takes part in a properly organised working bee. The record describes the activity, time, agreed verification and any permission to share it.

The person is more than the number of hours recorded. A C-Hour does not rank human worth or claim that every kind of work produces an identical outcome. It records a common measure of time while leaving the activity and its circumstances visible.

Reciprocity is a relationship. People may acknowledge each other, invite someone into a learning opportunity, share a meal, offer the use of a resource or support another person's project. Those choices remain voluntary. They do not settle a debt denominated in C-Hours.

## Keep four records distinct

| Record | What it describes | What follows from it |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution time | Verified voluntary human time and the activity involved | Recognition of the contribution |
| Money | Income, payments, grants, expenses and other financial transactions | The actual financial arrangement |
| Ownership | A documented interest in an enterprise or asset | Rights and responsibilities under that arrangement |
| Outcomes | What changed, for whom, and with what evidence | A basis for learning and assessing the work |

A person might have entries in all four. A paid trainer receives an agreed payment, helps an enterprise they partly own, volunteers at a separate gathering and supports a learner whose progress is later recorded. Those facts remain distinguishable. Combining them into one score would obscure how the programme works.

## A local example

Suppose a community film evening involves a paid technician and several volunteers. The technician's payment belongs in the event budget. The volunteers' agreed hours may be recorded as C-Hours. The event's attendance, accessibility, audience responses and financial result belong in its outcome and operating records.

Later, a local host might offer a voluntary thank-you gathering or a supported creative workshop. That is the host's freely chosen contribution to the relationship. It does not establish a conversion table between hours and tickets, food or services.

The same distinction applies to Mutual Futures. An enterprise's capacity to support learning or community activity comes from real resources and decisions. C-Hours help recognise contribution; they do not manufacture those resources or create a claim on the enterprise.

## Verification without making participation burdensome

An initial local record could be simple: who contributed, what happened, when it happened, how long it took, who confirmed it and what may be made public. The activity organiser and contributor would know the process before the work starts.

Corrections need an ordinary route. Someone may forget to log time, disagree about an entry or prefer to keep an activity private. A disputed entry deserves a human conversation and a visible correction history where appropriate. This is a record of shared work, rather than a permanent judgement on someone's character.

There is also a choice about what is worth recording. Informal care and everyday generosity have value even when nobody logs them. Participation in the recording system remains voluntary.

## What this means for the handover

Paul and any local group would be invited to use this reciprocal definition consistently in public explanations, software, grant material and operating agreements. A review of inherited material would identify older references to tokens, trading, cash conversion, redemption or financial backing and correct the explanation at its source.

Where an activity creates a paid role, its pay and conditions deserve their own agreement. Where a resource has a real cost, someone needs to budget for it. Where a programme offers support, the group needs to explain who provides it and how the decision is made.

This lets the reciprocity remain human and freely chosen, while the financial and organisational arrangements remain clear enough to sustain the work.

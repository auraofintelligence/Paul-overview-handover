This is also the clearest connection with your arts, culture and heritage portfolio and your background as an actor and facilitator. A public conversation about change needs people who understand how a story lands, who is missing from it and how a room becomes willing to participate.

## A local screen and media network

The [Minjerribah Screen and Media Network](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/minjerribah-screen-media-network/) is a proposed system for community communication, creative work, narrative moderation and outreach. It connects screens, phones, cinema and events with people producing and checking the material.

Its proposed workflow is straightforward: a tip or contribution comes in, someone checks it, an editor prepares it, the agreed version is published and broadcast, and the record is archived. Permission, source information and corrections travel with the work. A screen location needs a willing host, equipment, connectivity, maintenance and a clear person to call.

Narrative moderation means making the public story accountable to evidence, context and the people affected. It includes a correction route, a right to respond where appropriate, clear editorial responsibility and room for disagreement. A shared network needs published decisions about these matters, particularly when its participants also work with council or advocate a project.

## The Sand and Screen Hub

Public gatherings and a market are among the possible activities. They are future programme ideas. I have not run a market stall under Strange But True.

## Grain by Grain and Civilisation of Sand

[Grain by Grain](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/grain-by-grain-documentary/index.html) is a proposed 90-minute, seven-act documentary connecting local infrastructure and material capability with a much larger resilience horizon. It begins with tangible steps around Dunwich and follows the possible development of useful excavation, material testing, production and future settlements.

[Civilisation of Sand](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/civilisation-of-sand/) opens related questions through a simulation and learning world. Builders, resources, material passports and quests let people investigate how systems connect. The documentary and simulation have different jobs: one develops a public story; the other creates a place to explore choices. Neither stands in for engineering evidence.

Both belong in the cultural programme because the proposed future includes enjoyable daily life, creativity, play and belonging. An underground settlement intended for successive generations would need those qualities as deliberately as food, water and power.

## Information during a disruption

The same network could relay dated official advice and verified local service information when people need it. This would require a defined publishing process, backup arrangements and clear source attribution. The emergency information role belongs within agreed local disaster relationships.

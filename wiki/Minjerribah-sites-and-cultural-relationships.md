Minjerribah is home and the starting place for this programme's local expression. The proposed network brings together culture, living, learning, care, making and shared infrastructure across several places. Each place has its own people, history, authority and practical conditions.

I am developing part of this work with a local Indigenous clever man who is Oodgeroo Noonuccal, Kath Walker's, grandson. That relationship matters to the work. It does not make me a cultural authority or establish agreement from every Quandamooka person, family or organisation.

The [multi-site Minjerribah network](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/multi-site-Minjerribah-network) sets out the current proposal. It is a starting point for place-specific agreements, rather than a single agreement covering all land, services and cultural material.

## Different places, different purposes

| Place or proposal | Intended role | Important next question |
|---|---|---|
| 7-Mile | Noonuccal-led living, cultural activity, gardens, making, learning and residencies | Who holds each decision and what land, operating and cultural agreements apply? |
| 82 Claytons Road, Amity | Proposed gift and partnership involving aged care, disability support and Genesis-related research | Is there a willing land and institutional partnership, with suitable service providers? |
| 9 Ballow Road | Proposed Trust Hub and a home for shared working arrangements | What site access and hosting arrangement is available? |
| 10-12 Ballow Road | Sand and Screen Hub for sport, film, learning, gatherings and intergenerational activity | What site, design and community process would carry it? |
| Recovery and support places | Distinct proposals including men's recovery and places supporting women and children | Which qualified organisations and community members would lead each service? |

These proposals have connections, but they are separate commitments. The 9 Ballow Road Trust Hub and the 10-12 Ballow Road Sand and Screen Hub are different concepts. A promise or permission for one does not apply automatically to the other.

## 7-Mile and Indigenous majority ownership

The working commercial proposal for 7-Mile includes a 41/39/20 ownership split that retains an Indigenous majority. The broader direction also includes a purpose-specific not-for-profit structure with Indigenous majority control. The final arrangements would need the participating people's own decisions and proper documentation.

That proposed split belongs to the specified venture. It is not a formula imposed on every site or a claim over Country. The rights and responsibilities connected with Country, cultural knowledge and family relationships are not company assets created by an ownership chart.

The site material includes desktop measurements and early layout ideas. They help people discuss possibilities. Survey, ecology, fire access, servicing, heritage, accommodation and land-use decisions remain separate work. Early capacity sketches are too preliminary to describe as an approved development yield.

## 82 Claytons Road

The [Anglican partnership proposal](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/Anglican-Diocese-82-Claytons-Amity/) explores a gift for generations and a relationship between place, care and local capability. The proposed Athelas role brings aged care, disability support and Genesis-related research into view. Each of these activities has different operating requirements. Accommodation, funded disability services, aged care and health research need their own providers, agreements and support. A land partnership would begin with the actual landholder and institutions involved. The present proposal does not establish a completed gift, operating service or Anglican endorsement.

## Cultural relationships are part of daily practice

The practical questions occur at every step. Who is entitled to tell a story? Who decides whether it is recorded, translated or published? Who sees the original? What happens if a custodian changes a decision? Who is paid for cultural work? How does a young person learn through a relationship with the people concerned?

Those questions connect directly with the screen network, oral history work, living twin and AI tools. A broad consent form cannot answer every later use. A specific record of what was agreed, with whom and for which purpose gives people something concrete to revisit.

## Possibilities you might choose to explore

The first local step is a conversation with the people actually involved at each proposed place. It would establish interest, priorities, authority and a useful next piece of work. You could help connect relevant council officers, cultural organisations, potential service partners and resources where the participants want that support.

These proposals would be part of the optional stream of inputs alongside your usual work. You choose which interest you and whether to explore them further. The programme's coherence comes from useful connections between places while each place keeps its own voice and decisions.
